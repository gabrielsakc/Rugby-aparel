05 / The Crest

The Red Rose, in 8,400 stitches.

Every England garment carries an embroidered Red Rose at the heart — never printed, never sublimated. The rose is rendered in six thread colours on a Tajima TMEZ machine, with a satin under-stitch on the reverse so the inside finishes as cleanly as the outside.

Each rose takes between 12 and 16 minutes to complete. The serial number sewn into the inner seam guarantees lifetime repair — torn collars, frayed hems, missing buttons. Send the garment to the atelier; it returns mended, no charge.

Heritage / Filed 1871

We did not invent this game. We filed it. Every other claim is a footnote.

Rugby union was transmitted from English soil in 1871, when England met Scotland at Raeburn Place in the first international. The forty minutes of mud and intent that followed set the terms for everything that came after. Twickenham — opened in 1909, capacity 82,000 — is where the ledger is still kept.

The white jersey with the Red Rose is the most restrained uniform in the game. No hoops, no stripes, no overdesign. Just the rose — embroidered in six thread colours — and an obligation to the ledger. This catalogue is the wardrobe of that obligation.

07 / Twickenham

The home of rugby — SW13.

Twickenham is not simply a stadium. It is the document where English rugby files its intentions — an 82,000-seat archive of everything the union has won, lost, and learned since the first match played on 2 October 1909. Every international since has been a new entry in the same ledger.

The white jersey with the red rose is the most restrained uniform in the game. No hoops, no stripes. Just the rose — embroidered at the heart — and an obligation to show up dressed for it. We make the wardrobe of that afternoon.

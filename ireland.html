05 / The crest

The Shamrock, in 8,220 stitches.

The shamrock does not announce itself. It simply grows — through the cracks in stone walls, between the painted lines of a pitch, in the space between the posts at Lansdowne Road when the crowd goes silent right before the kick. That is the symbol we embroider: not a crest of dominance, but one of persistence. Each stitch is set by Tajima TMEZ machine, matte rayon thread, colour PMS 362 C — the IRFU reference since the union was constituted in 1879.

IRFU · THE SHAMROCK · EST. 1879

The Irish do not play to win. They play to make the game unforgettable.

In 1875, Ireland played their first international match against England. One hundred and forty-eight years later, they held the World No.1 ranking — a position earned not through institution but through relentless collective discipline. Four Grand Slams: 1948, 2009, 2018, 2023. Each one arrived when few expected it. That is the Irish way of filing evidence.

Brian O'Driscoll. Ronan O'Gara. Jonathan Sexton. Peter O'Mahony. Their names are not decoration — they are coordinates. We do not embroider their names on our garments. We embroider the symbol that called them to the field.

07 / Aviva Stadium

Lansdowne Road — the oldest test ground.

Dublin 4. Lansdowne Road. The oldest international rugby ground in the world, first used in 1872, rebuilt and reopened as the Aviva Stadium in 2010. 51,700 seats. On an autumn Saturday, when Ireland take on New Zealand or South Africa, the stands do not cheer — they roar in a pitch so low and so continuous it arrives in your chest before your ears. That roar is what we file.

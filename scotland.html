05 / The crest

The Thistle, in 7,840 stitches.

The thistle became Scotland's national emblem because, as the legend holds, a Norse raider stepped on one barefoot at night and cried out — waking the Scottish camp in time to defend. A symbol born from pain that became a warning. We embroider it not as decoration but as transmission: this garment came from somewhere specific, made by people who know what that place means. Each point set by Tajima TMEZ machine, matte rayon thread, colour PMS 2955 C — the SRU's registered navy since 1873.

SRU · THE THISTLE · EST. 1873

Scotland did not invent this game. Scotland played it first — and that is a different thing entirely.

On 27 March 1871, at Raeburn Place in Edinburgh, Scotland faced England in the first ever rugby international. Scotland won. One hundred and fifty-four years later, that fact still sits in the record. The SRU was founded two years later, in 1873, and has since produced players and coaches whose discipline has shaped how this game is played at every level: from the club pitches of the Borders to the turf of Murrayfield.

Gavin Hastings. Jim Telfer. Gordon Brown. Stuart Hogg. Finn Russell. The names change. The Thistle does not.

07 / Murrayfield

Edinburgh's fortress — EH12.

Murrayfield Road, Edinburgh EH12. Opened in 1925, expanded and rebuilt across ten decades until it became the largest rugby stadium in Europe — 67,144 seats. On autumn internationals, when the ground fills before kick-off against New Zealand or South Africa, the low cloud comes in from the Pentland Hills and the noise moves differently than anywhere else. That is the ground these garments belong to.
